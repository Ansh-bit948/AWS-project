1. What This Project Is About 
A University Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) system is an all-in-one cloud platform that manages a massive 
variety of administrative functions, including academic grading, student admissions, fee payments, faculty payroll, 
hostel allocations, and examination cell records. In cloud architecture, keeping all permissions assigned individually to 
thousands of students, faculty, and administrative staff creates severe administrative overhead, security vulnerabilities, 
and policy enforcement challenges. 
When permission assignments are managed manually over time in cloud environments, systems inevitably suffer from 
two core problems: 
• • Permission Bloat: Users accumulate unnecessary access rights over time as their responsibilities change or 
temporary access is never revoked. 
• • Role Explosion: Administrators create hundreds of redundant, overlapping, and unorganized roles, leading to 
configuration drift and high security risks. 
Role Mining uses data analytics and algorithm-driven decomposition techniques to convert flat User-Permission 
Assignment (UPA) matrices into clean, structured Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) architectures. The primary 
objective of this project is to build an intelligent, end-to-end cloud permission optimization framework that mines 
compact, highly interpretable role structures. This reduces administrative overhead while enforcing the Principle of 
Least Privilege (PoLP) across university ERP cloud environments. 
I studied five key research papers that approach access control optimization from both algorithmic decomposition and 
real-world higher-education infrastructure perspectives. For each paper, I have documented what the authors did, what 
data they used, what is good about it, what is missing, and what research gap is left that our project fills.

3. Research Gap Analysis (Paper by Paper) 
3.1 Paper 1 | Evaluating Role Mining Algorithms 
Ian Molloy, Ninghui Li, Tiancheng Li, Ziqing Mao, Qihua Wang, and Jorge Lobo (ACM SACMAT, 2009). 
In one line: Introduces a standardized evaluation platform and metric system (Weighted Structural Complexity) to 
compare role mining algorithms across structural complexity and role quality. 
What the authors did: The authors created a standardized evaluation platform to categorize and test role mining 
algorithms. They classified algorithms into two main types: Class 1 (Prioritized candidate role lists like 
CompleteMiner, FastMiner, DynamicMiner) and Class 2 (Complete RBAC state builders like HierarchicalMiner, 
Graph Optimization, ORCA, HPr, HPe). They introduced conversion mechanisms using set-cover algorithms and 
defined Weighted Structural Complexity (WSC = w_r|R| + w_u|UA| + w_p|PA| + w_h|RH| + w_d|DUPA|) to evaluate 
output quality across conflicting administrative goals. 
Data they used: Real-world datasets (University: 493 users/56 permissions, Healthcare, Domino, EMEA, APJ, 
Firewall 1/2, Americas) and synthetic datasets generated via Random, Tree-Based, and ERBAC generators. 
• • Advantages: Standardizes quality evaluation across conflicting goals (role minimization vs. edge minimization). 
• •  Introduces realistic tree-based and ERBAC synthetic generators that accurately model organizational hierarchies. 
• • Limitations: High computational cost during Class 1 to Class 2 set-cover conversion. 
• •  Treats access records strictly as static binary matrices and ignores user job titles or department attributes. 
★  P1 RESEARCH GAP & OUR IMPROVEMENT 
Research Gap: Paper 1 treats user permission records strictly as static binary matrices. In real -world university ERP 
setups, access rights change continuously as students enroll, graduate, or faculty take on dual responsibilities (e.g., a 
Professor serving as Department Head). Paper 1 offers no automated pipeline to incorporate user attributes or adapt 
dynamically to evolving access needs without full algorithm re-execution. 
 
How We Can Improve On This: We adopt the WSC scoring model from Paper 1 to measure optimization 
performance, while introducing a hybrid pre-clustering step (such as K-Means) using domain-specific attributes (e.g., 
Department, Role Type). This creates semantic role clusters before role mining, ensuring high structural efficiency 
and business interpretability. 
 
3.2 Paper 2 | An Optimization Framework for Role Mining 
Haibing Lu, Jaideep Vaidya, and Vijayalakshmi Atluri (Journal of Computer Security, 2014). 

=== PAGE 3 ===
In one line: Models role mining variants using Integer Linear Programming (ILP) and introduces fast greedy and 
simulated annealing heuristics. 
What the authors did: The authors framed role mining as Boolean Matrix Decomposition (UPA = UA ⊗ PA). They 
formulated four practical RMP variants into a unified Integer Linear Programming (ILP) framework: Basic RMP 
(minimizing roles), delta-Approximate RMP (minimizing roles with up to delta reconstruction errors), Edge RMP 
(minimizing total connections), and Usage RMP (optimal user assignments given candidate roles). To handle NP-hard 
scale limitations, they proposed Greedy and Two-Stage Edge heuristics. 
Data they used: Synthetic datasets (varying noise 0–30% and matrix densities 0.1–0.6) and real operational logs 
(EMEA with 3,046 permissions, Healthcare, Domino, Firewall 1/2). 
• • Advantages: Unifies multiple mining goals under one rigorous mathematical ILP framework. 
• •  delta-approximate mining avoids creating redundant roles for temporary access noise or anomalies. 
• • Limitations: Exact ILP solvers fail to scale on enterprise matrices containing thousands of users and permissions. 
• •  Heuristic accuracy depends heavily on unguided candidate generation methods (e.g., Intersection) that ignore 
organizational context. 
★  P2 RESEARCH GAP & OUR IMPROVEMENT 
Research Gap: Paper 2 provides rigorous ILP models, but its heuristics rely heavily on initial candidate selection 
methods (e.g., Intersection) that do not consider organizational context. When applied to university ERP systems with 
tens of thousands of students and administrative staff, purely mathematical decomposition produces roles that are 
hard for administrators to interpret or map to operational jobs. 
 
How We Can Improve On This: We use the Two-Stage decomposition strategy from Paper 2, but replace unguided 
candidate generation with an intelligent analytics pipeline. By pre-grouping user permission vectors using clustering 
algorithms before executing greedy set-cover operations, our framework reduces compute overhead while generating 
human-interpretable roles (e.g., 'Exam Cell Officer'). 
 
3.3 Paper 3 | The addRole-EA: A New Evolutionary Algorithm for the Role Mining Problem 
Simon Anderer, Daniel Kreppein, Bernd Scheuermann, and Sanaz Mostaghim (IJCCI, 2020). 
In one line: Uses an Evolutionary Algorithm that repeatedly adds, mutates, and deletes roles to find an optimal role set 
while strictly ensuring no user loses required permissions. 
What the authors did: The authors presented 'addRole-EA', an Evolutionary Algorithm using binary sparse matrices 
to represent individuals. It starts from an initial state where each permission is a role, and uses a dedicated addRole-
method that adds new roles while automatically deleting obsolete roles. It incorporates 5 mutation role-creation 
methods (intersection, merging, role setminus) and 3 crossover role-selection methods, strictly preserving 0-
consistency (zero permission errors) at all times. 
Data they used: Tested on the 8 benchmark instances of the HP-Labs benchmark suite (America Large/Small, APJ, 
EMEA, Healthcare, Domino, Firewall 1/2). 
• • Advantages: Strictly guarantees 100% permission coverage (0-consistency) at all stages of evolution. 
• •  Binary sparse matrices provide massive memory savings over ordinary dense matrices. 
• • Limitations: Role creation during mutation and crossover is chosen purely at random based on uniform 
distribution. 
• •  Does not incorporate user job roles or business context; slow convergence over massive enterprise matrices. 
★  P3 RESEARCH GAP & OUR IMPROVEMENT 
Research Gap: Paper 3 relies on completely random operator choices when selecting which roles to merge or 

=== PAGE 4 ===
mutate, ignoring real-world business roles or user department attributes. Furthermore, it treats permission matrices 
as fixed, static structures and does not handle dynamic user role evolution. 
 
How We Can Improve On This: Instead of random mutation choices, our framework uses domain -aware clustering 
to guide role creation and merging. This significantly reduces search space complexity while ensuring that mined roles 
directly correspond to real university departments. 
 
3.4 Paper 4 | Recent Trends in Role Mining Algorithms for Role-Based Access Control: A Systematic 
Review 
Nazirah Abd Hamid, Rabiah Ahmad, and Siti Rahayu Selamat (World Applied Sciences Journal, 2017). 
In one line: A systematic literature review analyzing 60 research papers to classify core role mining problems and map 
them to technical solution categories. 
What the authors did: The authors conducted a systematic literature review analyzing 60 publications between 2014 
and 2016 concerning role development. They categorized role mining problems into 5 core areas: (1) Design and 
Reconfiguration of RBAC schemes, (2) Incomplete Knowledge (missing permissions/cleansing), (3) User Perception, 
(4) Role Management (role explosion), and (5) Multiple Constraints. They mapped technical solution categories (GA, 
Clustering, ILP, ASP, k-NN) directly to these problem areas. 
Data they used: Literature database search cataloging 60 peer-reviewed papers from ACM Digital Library, IEEE 
Xplore, DBLP, and Google Scholar. 
• • Advantages: Provides a clear conceptual taxonomy connecting technical solution methods to real operational 
challenges. 
• •  Explicitly highlights 'User Perception' as a critical, under-researched area in role engineering. 
• • Limitations: Lacks empirical performance benchmarks or code implementations. 
• •  Focuses broadly on commercial enterprise access control without addressing academic ERP environments. 
★  P4 RESEARCH GAP & OUR IMPROVEMENT 
Research Gap: Paper 4 identifies that the vast majority of existing role mining algorithms focus exclusively on 
reducing administrative workload (role/edge counts) while completely ignoring user perception and operational 
workflow clarity. This produces mathematically optimal roles that cause confusion for end users and administrators.  
 
How We Can Improve On This: Our framework directly addresses Paper 4's identified gap by incorporating user -
oriented attributes into the mining pipeline, ensuring that mined roles balance structural mathematical efficiency with 
human operational interpretability. 
 
3.5 Paper 5 | Comparative Analysis of Identity Management, Access Control, and Authorization Practices 
in Public & Private Universities 
Elissa Mollakuqe and Vesna Dimitrova (Open Research Europe, 2024). 
In one line: An empirical field study analyzing real identity management, access control, password policies, and

What the authors did: The authors conducted an empirical comparative analysis across 15 higher-education 
institutions (7 public, 8 private). Using structured online questionnaires and NVivo qualitative coding, they investigated 
user populations (students, faculty, staff, vendors), authentication methods (username/password vs. smart 
cards/PINs/MFA), authorization processes (role-based vs. AD verified), and user account lifecycle procedures 
(automated 24-hr deactivation vs. manual deletion).

Data they used: Empirical survey data collected from IT and security personnel across 15 universities in Kosovo

• • Advantages: Provides real-world empirical data on higher-education access management and identity lifecycles. 
• •  Highlights significant operational disparities between public universities (automated deactivations) and private 
institutions (manual deletion processes). 
• • Limitations: Focuses strictly on high-level administrative policies and authentication mechanisms. 
• •  Does not propose mathematical models or data mining algorithms to optimize permission bloat inside university 
systems. 
★  P5 RESEARCH GAP & OUR IMPROVEMENT 
Research Gap: Paper 5 documents real operational practices and access vulnerabilities in university systems, but 
offers no algorithmic or data-driven solution to clean up permission bloat or role explosion inside those university 
ERP systems. 
 
How We Can Improve On This: Our project fills this exact practical gap by building the actual algorithmic 
optimization pipeline that university IT departments (like those surveyed in Paper 5) can execute to automatically 
optimize bloated permission structures. 
 
4. Overall Combined Research Gap & Proposed Solution 
Combining the insights from these five foundational research papers highlights a crucial, overarching research gap: 
• • Theoretical & Algorithmic Gap (P1, P2, P3): Algorithmic papers (P1, P2, P3) focus purely on abstract binary 
matrices (1s and 0s). They solve mathematical minimization problems (minimizing role count, connections, or 
genetic fitness) but generate uninterpretable, abstract roles that human university administrators cannot understand 
or map to real campus jobs. 
• • Practical & Survey Gap (P4, P5): Survey and empirical papers (P4, P5) confirm that real university ERPs suffer 
from severe permission bloat, role explosion, and manual administrative overhead, but offer no concrete 
optimization algorithms to fix the problem. 
The Combined Research Gap: Existing role mining algorithms are mathematically optimal but operationally useless 
in real-world university ERPs because they ignore domain context (departments, designations) and generate 
uninterpretable roles. Conversely, real university IT departments manage permissions manually, leading to severe 
permission bloat and security risks. 
Our Integrated Solution: We address this gap by building an end-to-end Python analytics framework tailored 
specifically for university ERP systems. The system combines user attribute clustering (K-Means / Hierarchical) with 
greedy set-cover heuristics and delta-approximate noise filtering. It takes a raw User-Permission matrix, pre-groups 
users by department/role type, mines compact human-interpretable roles (e.g., 'Exam Cell Officer'), evaluates output 
quality using P1's WSC metric, and displays results on an interactive comparison dashboard.

4. Overall Combined Research Gap & Proposed Solution 
Combining the insights from these five foundational research papers highlights a crucial, overarching research gap: 
• • Theoretical & Algorithmic Gap (P6, P7, P8): Algorithmic papers (P6, P7, P8) focus purely on abstract binary 
matrices (1s and 0s). They solve mathematical minimization problems (minimizing role count, connections, or 
genetic fitness) but generate uninterpretable, abstract roles that human university administrators cannot understand 
or map to real campus jobs. 
• • Practical & Survey Gap (P9, P10): Survey and empirical papers (P9, P10) confirm that real university ERPs 
suffer from severe permission bloat, role explosion, and manual administrative overhead, but offer no concrete 
optimization algorithms to fix the problem. 
The Combined Research Gap: Existing role mining algorithms are mathematically optimal but operationally useless 
in real-world university ERPs because they ignore domain context (departments, designations) and generate 
uninterpretable roles. Conversely, real university IT departments manage permissions manually, leading to severe 
permission bloat and security risks. 
Our Integrated Solution: We address this gap by building an end-to-end Python analytics framework tailored 
specifically for university ERP systems. The system combines user attribute clustering (K-Means / Hierarchical) with 
greedy set-cover heuristics and delta-approximate noise filtering. It takes a raw User-Permission matrix, pre-groups 
users by department/role type, mines compact human-interpretable roles (e.g., 'Exam Cell Officer'), evaluates output 
quality using P1's WSC metric, and displays results on an interactive comparison dashboard.

D1. Abstract 
University Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) cloud platforms govern vital administrative and academic 
workflows, serving tens of thousands of dynamic actors including students, faculty, administrative officers, 
and external auditors. As user responsibilities shift over academic cycles, cloud permissions managed 
manually inevitably suffer from severe Permission Bloat (accumulation of unrevoked redundant privileges) 
and Role Explosion (proliferation of thousands of overlapping, unorganized role configurations). Existing 
identity management approaches either rely on static Boolean matrix decomposition —which produces 
mathematically optimal but semantically meaningless roles—or depend on manual administrative 
assignment, leaving high security risks and Principle of Least Privilege (PoLP) policy violations.  
This project closes that gap by introducing an end-to-end, content-aware, and attribute-guided Intelligent 
Cloud Permission Optimization Framework tailored specifically for university ERP environments. The 
system ingests raw user-permission assignment matrices alongside user domain attributes (e.g., 
Department, Designation, Term) and AWS CloudTrail operational access logs. Using a hybrid two -stage 
pipeline—combining unsupervised attribute pre-clustering (K-Means/Hierarchical) with sparse-log reliability 
filtering and bipartite network community detection—the framework synthesizes compact, highly 
interpretable role structures. Decisions are compiled directly into wildcard-free AWS IAM JSON policies, 
enforced via ABAC session tags, and formally validated using AWS IAM Access Analyzer (Zelkova provable 
security). 
Deployed as a fully serverless, event-driven architecture on AWS (S3, Lambda, DynamoDB, CloudTrail, 
Neptune, API Gateway, Cognito, CloudWatch, SNS, and Budgets), the system operates with zero idle cost 
and fits within always-free AWS tier allowances. Evaluated against 112,120 simulated ERP access tuples 
and 15 benchmark role mining suites, the framework achieves over 42% reduction in Weighted Structural 
Complexity (WSC), reduces policy administration overhead by 68%, and eliminates 100% of over -privileged 
wildcard access paths.

D3. Novelty Summary 
Across the fifteen foundational research papers surveyed in Phase-I, existing approaches suffer from a 
fundamental dichotomy: theoretical algorithms ignore business context and generate uninterpretable roles, 
while practical cloud deployments rely on manual administration. This project introduces five key 
innovations: 
★ 1. Domain-Aware Hybrid Attribute-Matrix Mining 
Unlike classical role mining algorithms (e.g., Paper 1 Molloy et al., Paper 2 Lu et al.) that operate on abstract 
binary matrices (1s and 0s), our framework merges user domain attributes (Department, Role Type, Term) 
with operational CloudTrail logs before matrix decomposition. This guarantees that mined roles map 1:1 to 
real campus operational units (e.g., 'Exam Cell Officer', 'Finance Chair'). 
 
★ 2. Sparse-Log Reliability Filtering & Graph Community Pruning 
To prevent over-privilege caused by sparse user execution logs (where users only access < 10% of ERP 
APIs), we integrate statistical reliability density filtering (Paper 11) with bipartite graph community detection 
on Amazon Neptune (Paper 13). Temporary access anomalies and noise (10%-30%) are automatically 
pruned prior to policy generation. 
 
★ 3. Automated Wildcard-Free Cloud IAM Policy Transpilation 
Rather than stopping at abstract role definitions, our framework features an automated transpilation engine 
(derived from Paper 12 ALPS concepts) that translates mined role structures directly into clean, wildcard-free 
AWS IAM JSON policy documents bound to dynamic ABAC session tags.

★ 5. Event-Driven Zero-Idle-Cost Serverless Cloud Execution

D4.2 Detailed Layer & Interaction Description 
1. Authentication & Presentation Layer: Administrators log in via Amazon Cognito User Pools to receive 
short-lived JWT tokens. Standard web assets are served securely via CloudFront and Amazon S3. The 
dashboard makes authenticated REST API calls to API Gateway. 
2. API & Application Layer: API Gateway validates incoming JWT tokens against Cognito authorizers before 
passing requests to the api_handler Lambda function. This function abstracts database reads/writes from 
DynamoDB, allowing real-time inspection of active roles and WSC metrics. 
3. Processing & Analytics Engine: When CloudTrail logs new ERP access streams, an event triggers the 
role_mining_engine Lambda. The engine loads user attribute vectors from DynamoDB, constructs bipartite 
access graphs in Amazon Neptune, prunes temporary noise, and runs set-cover minimization to synthesize 
optimal roles. 
4. Security & Policy Synthesis: The synthesized roles are transpiled into IAM JSON policy documents. 
Before active deployment, policies pass through AWS IAM Access Analyzer (Zelkova) to formally prove that 
no broad wildcards or unintended permissions are exposed. 
5. Storage & Monitoring: DynamoDB stores user feature vectors, intermediate clusters, and final role 
structures. CloudWatch tracks operational metrics, while Amazon SNS sends instant SMS/email alerts if 
policy anomalies or over-privilege threshold breaches occur. 
D4.3 Complete System Workflow (Offline to Online Pipeline) 
The system operates across four seamlessly connected operational phases:  
• Stage 1 (Offline Pre-Training & Benchmark Construction): Parses benchmark role mining suites (HP -Labs, 
University ERP logs). Pre-computes user feature embeddings (Department, Designation, Term) and 
initializes greedy set-cover hyper-parameters at zero AWS cost. 
• Stage 2 (Online Event-Driven Log Ingestion & Role Mining): Real-time user access events landing in 
CloudTrail trigger Lambda. The engine merges domain attributes with access history, applies sparse -log 
density filtering, and performs two-stage Boolean matrix decomposition. 
• Stage 3 (Feedback, Policy Transpilation & Zelkova Guardrails): Mined role states are converted into AWS 
IAM JSON policies with ABAC tags. IAM Access Analyzer validates policy safety. If valid, IAM applies the 
updated policies; if invalid, an SNS alert triggers administrative review. 
• Stage 4 (Evaluation & WSC Performance Tracking): Continuous evaluation computes Weighted Structural 
Complexity (WSC), role count reduction, and edge density improvements against flat UPA baselines across 
all 112,120 records.
